# Routine — Domain

Concepts and vocabulary specific to this module. Terms listed here either only exist within this module's context or carry a different meaning from the shared [[domain]].

## Routine

A standing instruction to do a piece of work again and again. A routine holds three things: what the work is, how often it comes round, and who does it.

A routine is not a task. It is never started and never closed, because it describes work rather than being work. It makes tasks — see **occurrence** below.

A routine belongs to exactly one household, like everything else in Homehood.

## Occurrence

One turn of a routine: the task the routine makes for one date.

An occurrence **is** a task. Everything the shared [[domain]] says about a task, and everything in [[tasks/rules]], is true of it with no exception — it has a requester, an executor and a task state, it is commented on, started, closed, archived or deleted exactly as any other task. "Occurrence" is the word for its relationship to the routine, not a second kind of task.

We do not say "instance" or "repeat".

## Schedule

How often a routine comes round. A schedule is one of:

| Schedule | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Every day | One occurrence a day |
| Every *n* days | One occurrence every *n* days, counted from the start date |
| Chosen days of the week | One occurrence on each chosen day — Monday and Thursday, or only Sunday |
| Every month, on a chosen date | One occurrence a month, on that date |

A routine also has a **start date**: the date of its first occurrence. A routine has no end date.

## Due date

The date an occurrence belongs to. The schedule gives it.

A due date is a date, not a time. An occurrence is due on Tuesday, not at seven on Tuesday.

## Routine state

A routine is in exactly one state at a time:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Active | It makes an occurrence on every date its schedule gives |
| Paused | It makes none. Everything it holds is kept, and it can become active again |
| Ended | It will make no more, ever. Kept for what it holds |

`Ended` is the end of a routine the household used and does not want any more. It is kept rather than deleted because by then it carries history: the occurrences it made, who did them, and the misses.

A routine that never made an occurrence is deleted, not ended. There is nothing in it worth keeping. This mirrors [[tasks/rules#archiving-ends-a-task-that-was-started]].

Deleting a routine removes it. Deleting is not a state.

## Miss

An occurrence that nobody started and nobody closed, found waiting when the routine makes the next one.

A miss is recorded on the routine. It is not a task and not a task state — the waiting occurrence is deleted, and the routine keeps the fact that the work did not happen on that date.

A miss cannot be done, closed or undone afterwards. Yesterday is not available any more. See [[decisions/0010-one-occurrence-waits-at-a-time]].

An occurrence somebody started is never a miss. Work that was begun is real work, and it is left alone.

## Rotation

An ordered list of two or more members who take a routine in turn. A routine either names a single executor or holds a rotation — never both.

A rotation is how the household says "we share this". It is the answer to "whose turn is it", which is the recurring form of the question the product exists to answer.

## Turn

Whose occurrence the next one is. On a routine with a single executor the turn never moves. On a routine with a rotation the turn moves one place down the list every time an occurrence is made, and starts again at the top when it runs off the end.

The turn moves when the occurrence is made, not when it is closed. A missed occurrence does not give a member a second turn — see [[decisions/0011-a-routine-names-one-executor-or-a-rotation]].

## A member on a routine

Shorthand used by the rules. A member is **on** a routine when they are its requester, its executor, or in its rotation.

It matters because a routine with a former member on it pauses — see [[rules#a-routine-with-a-former-member-on-it-pauses]].
